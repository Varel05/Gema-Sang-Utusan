# Chapter 1: Embers by the Forest Verge

Night over the border valley always descended with solemn stillness, broken only by the creak of cedarwood walls and the whisper of mountain drafts rustling through fallow fields.

In the corner of a modest stilt chamber, a single oil lamp cast a gentle amber glow across a low round table. Thin ribbons of pine-resin smoke curled toward the rafters, weaving through the shadows of a silver-bearded elder and a little girl sitting cross-legged upon a woven pandan mat.

"Grandpa... did the Envoy really once set foot on the very ground we walk on?" whispered young Mira. Her bright, earnest eyes were fixed on the deep lines carved into her grandfather’s weathered face—the Village Elder, guardian of the settlement's oldest oral memories.

The old man offered a warm, comforting smile. His calloused fingers brushed gently through his granddaughter's hair before he sprinkled a pinch of dried herbs into a clay bowl.

"Far more than merely walking, Mira," his voice rumbled low and rhythmic, like a bedtime tale passed down through hundreds of turnings of the seasons. "The Envoy was woven directly from the natural laws of this world—from the very heartbeat of Aethelgard itself. Unlike the Archangel, who was wrought from pure light in the highest heavens, the Envoy walked among mortal men to guide them in order, balance, and quiet reverence toward the Creator."

Mira pulled her knees to her chest, hugging her cold shins. "Then... why did the old world fall apart, Grandpa?"

The Elder’s chest rose and fell in a heavy sigh. The lamp's flame flickered as a draft slipped through the wooden slats.

"Because mankind allowed greed to poison the earth," the old man answered softly. "They fought for thrones, spilled each other's blood, and desecrated the soil entrusted into their keeping. When heaven's warnings went unheeded, the Archangel descended to enact a great cleansing. Magnificent cities crumbled, swallowed whole by divine reckoning."

"But our ancestors survived..." Mira murmured.

"Our ancestors refused to take part in that slaughter," the Elder replied, gazing at her with tender warmth. "They chose instead to tend the land, cherish their kin, and keep their prayers pure. That is why we are called the ones who remained—*The Remnant*. As for those who lost themselves to bitter strife... it is told they were cast into a hidden sanctuary beyond the bounds of the cosmos, so the mortal seed would not perish entirely from existence."

The Elder patted her shoulder gently and rose to his feet. "It grows late, Mira. Close your eyes. Always remember the path of our forebears: tend the soil that feeds you, and shield those who cry out for aid."

Blowing softly across the wick, he left only a glowing red ember before stepping out and drawing the timber door shut.

The room settled into the silver gloom of moonlight filtering through the window lattice. Mira lay down, pulling her coarse wool blanket up to her chin. In her mind, luminous angels and cosmic secrets still spun in quiet wonder.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

A faint, steady rap against the window shutter startled her from her reverie.

Mira held her breath, listening intently. The knock sounded again, accompanied by an unmistakable whisper through the crack in the timber.

"Mira! Pssst... Mira! You're still awake, aren't you?"

Rising quietly, Mira crept across the chill floorboards on bare feet and unlatched the wooden shutter with utmost care.

Outside, perched precariously on a sturdy limb of the old apple tree beside the house, a boy's cheerful face beamed under the pale moonlight. His unruly brown hair stood in tousled tufts, and a hunting cape of cured hide hung over his shoulders. It was Kael.

"Kael?!" Mira whispered in frantic disbelief, leaning out through the casement. "What are you doing here in the middle of the night? If Grandpa or your father wakes up, you'll get in so much trouble!"

"Relax, they're dead to the world," Kael whispered eagerly, his hazel eyes widening with suppressed excitement. "Come with me to Pine Cliff! Right now!"

"To Pine Cliff? In the middle of the night? Have you lost your mind?"

"Just listen!" Kael swung down from the branch with feline grace, landing on the timber veranda without a creak. He leaned forward, utterly in earnest. "Father and Mother were talking about it at sundown while cleaning the snares. Tonight, a falling star is going to cross the valley sky!"

Mira furrowed her brow. "You woke me up just for a falling star?"

"It's no ordinary star!" Kael whispered, his hands clenching passionately against his chest. "Mother said the ancient hunters believed falling stars are angels streaking across the sky, carrying earth's news back to God! And Father said that if you whisper a prayer while watching one fall, the angel will hear your voice and bear it straight to the heavens. Your wish will surely come true!"

Kael’s eyes shone with wild wonder as he tugged at Mira's sleeve with desperate longing.

Hearing the words *angel* and *prayer*, the tale her grandfather had just spun echoed fresh in Mira's thoughts. The chill hesitation of the night slowly melted away, overtaken by her friend's boundless fervor.

"Just... just for a little bit, okay?" Mira whispered hesitantly. "The moment we see it, we come straight back."

"I swear it!" Kael grinned ear to ear. "Come on, this way!"

With Kael’s steady hand, Mira climbed over the sill and dropped lightly onto the damp grass. The two twelve-year-olds slipped like shadows through the sleeping farming village, darting past silent cattle pens and rustling wheat plots, heading toward the rugged trail that climbed into the forest verge.

---

Pine Cliff loomed stern and ancient at the far edge of the settlement. The sheer promontory was crowned by a stand of primeval pines smelling sharply of resin, overlooking the open sky and the sprawling expanse of the valley below. From this precipice, the night canopy of Aethelgard stretched unbroken by valley mists, dusted with thousands of stars like silver sand cast across velvet.

Kael and Mira sat side by side at the rocky brink, hugging their knees against the biting mountain wind.

"Look toward the east, Mira," Kael pointed toward a distant cluster of constellations. "Father said they usually cross from over there."

Mira tilted her head back, gazing into the vastness of the heavens. "Kael... if you see the star angel, what are you going to wish for?"

Kael patted the bone-handled hunting knife tucked into his belt with a proud grin. "I'm going to wish to be a brave guardian knight! So I can defend our whole village, look after Father and Mother, and protect you from every danger."

A faint blush touched Mira's cheeks in the cool dark. But before she could reply, the whispering pine breeze abruptly died.

Absolute silence.

It was not the peaceful quiet of an ordinary mountain night, but a heavy, unnatural stillness that weighed down the air. The rustle of branches and the chirping of crickets were snuffed out in a heartbeat—as though the forest itself had drawn a sharp breath and forgotten to release it. Even the wind died. The pines stopped swaying. The world around them became a held breath, a waiting darkness that pressed against Mira's eardrums like something about to break.

The temperature atop Pine Cliff plummeted. A bone-gnawing cold—dry, hollow, carrying the bitter stench of ozone and dead ash—crept down from the ancient pines behind them.

Beside her, Kael went rigid. The easy grin that had brightened his face vanished in an instant; the hand resting on his knee clamped fiercely around the hilt of his bone knife. His neck cords strained, and his breath hitched as though something invisible had seized his throat.

"Mira... get up. Get behind me," Kael breathed. His voice was taut and trembling, stripped clean of boyish warmth.

"Kael... it's so cold..." Mira shivered violently as the alien draft gripped her chest.

From the swirling grey fog between two colossal pines, something stepped into the open.

The figure stood over seven feet tall. Its silhouette mimicked the frame of a man, yet it moved with jarring, fractured hitches beneath the trees. Its skin was a dull, mottled grey-purple, like ash frozen into stone. What turned Kael’s blood to ice was its neck—the creature had no head; the empty hollow above its shoulders was replaced by a roaring, wild orb of dark violet flame that burned without fuel.

Across its chest and limbs, jagged fissures gaped open. Through the cracks, there was no flesh, no bone, no drop of blood—only a bottomless void licking with the same cold violet fire from deep within its hollow core.

This was no beast of the wild woods. The entity radiated an aura of unyielding hunger that withered the very air around it. The purple blaze atop its torso churned violently as it tilted upward, tasting the freezing draft upon the cliff, as though scenting the warm blood of the two children before it.

"Run, Mira! Run back to the village!" Kael shouted.

Drawing his bone knife, the boy stepped forward and threw his arms wide to shield Mira. His palms were slick with cold sweat, yet his boots dug stubbornly into the stone.

"No! I'm not leaving you alone!" Mira cried out. Her knees trembled, her chest aching from the suffocating chill.

The ash-grey monstrosity let out a hollow, guttural rasp from its core of fire. With a single backhand sweep of its elongated arm, it struck a nearby pine trunk, shattering the ancient wood into splinters with explosive force. It lunged forward, grey-purple talons outstretched straight toward Kael.

Above them in the boundless sky, not a single falling star crossed the dark. The heavens remained deaf and still. *They had come all this way to find an angel in the sky*, Mira thought wildly—and the sky had given them nothing but cold and monster and terror.

Yet Kael did not turn to flee. He dug his heels into the granite, fought down the tremor in his knees, and thrust his small bone blade straight at the oncoming terror. His breath caught in his throat, his mind screaming a single, wordless vow: *Not her... whatever happens, don't you touch Mira!*

At the same instant, Mira refused to surrender to despair. Seeing the monster barely an arm's length from Kael, sheer instinct overcame her terror. Her fingers scrambled across the rock, seizing a sharp, jagged stone, and hurled it with all her might at the violet flame above the creature's shoulders.

*Crack!*

The stone struck the edge of the monster's hollow neck-ring, scattering violet embers and deflecting its raking swipe just inches above Kael's neck.

Then, the earth beneath their feet answered.

It was not a tremor that fractured stone, but a deep, resonant pulse welling up from the bedrock of Pine Cliff. Fine, radiant cracks spidered across the moss and granite. From every crevice, brilliant plumes of light burst skyward.

*Fwoooosh!*

A roaring sphere of fire surged from a rock fracture, followed by half a dozen others erupting from the earth around them.

The flames were not common red or orange ember, but a blinding, pristine white fringed with vibrant cerulean fire. Kael's arm flew up to shield his eyes on instinct. The heat—*warmth*, pure and living—rolled against his skin in a wave, and for one disoriented heartbeat the boy could not understand what was happening. The searing cold that had gripped his chest since the monster stepped out of the dark began to dissolve. His knees buckled, not from fear this time, but from the sudden, overwhelming absence of dread. He stared between his fingers, wide-eyed and breathless, unable to look away.

Mira, at his side, had gone completely still. Not the stillness of terror—the stillness of pure, helpless wonder. The white-blue light fell across her tear-streaked cheeks. To her stunned eyes, it looked exactly as if the stars they had come to find had changed their minds and fallen here instead—not from above, but from the bones of the earth itself.

The hollow, freezing aura of the beast was instantly repelled by a wave of living warmth rolling off the white-blue fire.

The fiery orbs soared upward, then converged, spiraling violently between the children and the monster. The radiant white-blue glare bathed the entire summit of Pine Cliff in midday brilliance amidst the dead of night. Within the heart of that swirling vortex, particles of solidifying light condensed with astonishing speed—knitting together the shoulders, limbs, and form of a living youth.

The blinding radiance settled into a single, warm exhalation of mountain wind.

Standing steadfast before the children was a young man. His silver-white hair cascaded loosely, tipped with strokes of vivid azure at every strand, while his porcelain skin radiated a soft, living blue luminescence. His eyes held the quiet, steady glow of white-blue embers, staring out across the crags with blank, pristine stillness, as though touching the physical world for the very first time. His lips were firmly set, devoid of words.

When the ash-purple husk let out a feral roar and lunged once more with its jagged talons, the youth stepped forward.

Without weapon and without hesitation, he placed his body directly between the monster and the children.

*BOOOM!*

The creature's rake struck the bare palm of the youth's outstretched hand. The collision between freezing void and blazing order released a thunderous shockwave that hurled the monster backward into the cliff face. A piercing, agonizing shriek tore from the flame within its hollow shoulders before its ash-like shell fractured entirely, dissolving into a swirling plume of violet smoke that fled into the black abyss of the woods.

The horror vanished, leaving only a faint drift of grey ash settling on the midnight breeze.

Kael and Mira remained frozen in place. Their ears rang; their chests heaved in sheer shock. Before them, the youth remained standing with his back turned, motionless and silent.

Then, without the slightest warning, his upright posture wavered. The young man's body collapsed forward, hitting the pine-needle-strewn stone with a dull thud.

"Hey!" Kael gasped, his bone knife slipping from numb fingers and clattering onto the rock.

Mira was the first to react. Her terror gave way entirely to the healer's instinct. Dropping to her knees beside the unmoving stranger, her trembling fingers found the pulse at his neck, then pressed against his brow.

"Kael, his breath is so weak! His body is freezing cold!" Mira cried in panic. "He saved us... he got hurt protecting us!"

Quickly unfastening her small wool cloak, Mira draped it over the youth's chill chest.

"Wait here, Mira! Watch over him!" Kael yelled, spinning toward the descent. "I'm running to the village to get Father! Just hold on!"

Kael’s pounding footsteps faded rapidly into the dark, swallowed by the mountain path.

Now only Mira remained at the quiet crest of Pine Cliff. She knelt upon the stone, gently cradling the stranger's head in her lap. Warm tears spilled down her cheeks, falling upon his pale, dust-streaked face.

"Please... hold on..." Mira sobbed softly. "Kael is bringing help... don't go..."

Amidst the stillness of the crags and the gentle tremble of her voice against his ear, the young man's fingers twitched faintly against the rock. His eyelids fluttered, parting just enough to reveal the dim, white-blue embers of his eyes. From his bloodless lips slipped a faint, hoarse breath—the very first mortal sound woven by his newborn form:

"...Mi... ra..."

Mira’s breath caught in her throat. Her entire heart trembled to hear her own name spoken by the stranger's lips. The voice was raspy, nearly swallowed by the night wind, yet it carried a profound warmth that banished the chill from her chest.

"I'm here... I'm right here," Mira whispered, taking his cold hand between both of hers. "My name is Mira. Hold on... please, just keep breathing..."

The youth's eyes drifted shut once more as his consciousness slipped back into the quiet depths of exhaustion. Yet his cold fingers remained intertwined within Mira's grasp, beneath the silent vault of Pine Cliff, where the night had finally found its peace.
