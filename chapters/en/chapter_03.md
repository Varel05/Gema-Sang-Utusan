# Chapter 3: First Breath upon Mortal Soil

The sky in that ancient memory held no shade of blue.

There were no clouds, no whispering mountain winds, and no golden sun to warm the soil. What stretched across the firmament was an expanse of blinding, immaculate white—frigid, unyielding, and demanding total extinction. That radiance was no source of life; it was a sovereign decree demanding absolute cleansing.

From the torn vault of the split heavens stood a solitary entity woven from the fierce embers of celestial light. Its wings spanned the horizon, casting immaculate blades of flame downward to scour the dust deemed stained.

And beneath its feet... thousands of mortal cries crawled upon the shattered earth.

Those small beings of blood and bone wept, clutching their children in the churning mire, begging for a mercy already foreclosed by the decree of heaven.

He knew the essence of his own existence. He was not born of celestial light, but woven from the deep veins of the earth and the harmonious order of the realm where those mortals drew their breath.

And so, he stepped forward.

His arms rose against the falling sky. From his breast, he projected a towering bulwark—an invisible bastion braided from the primal laws of nature, shielding the weeping mortals at his back. When the descending blades of cleansing struck, the collision fractured the fabric of space itself.

The agony did not rend mortal flesh; it tore at the very fabric of his being.

Something deep within his core cracked. The barrage of holy light battered him without respite until a central fragment of his essence broke away—shorn off and cast downward like a spent cinder, buried deep within the belly of the earth. His remaining consciousness was drawn into a freezing, bottomless abyss, sealed behind endless walls alongside the muffled screams of those he had preserved.

Shattered. Hollow. Extinguished.

***

"Haaah...!"

A sharp, violent intake of breath dragged him out of the dark void.

He bolted upright upon a hard surface laid with dry straw. His chest heaved in frantic, ragged rhythms. A searing sensation burned in his throat—a dry, stinging, freezing friction unlike anything he had ever known.

Air. He was drawing physical air.

His eyes snapped open, catching motes of morning dust dancing in the golden beams slanting through the timber slats. The scent of pine resin, damp clay, and the sharp, medicinal tang of dried herbs struck his senses in an overwhelming wave.

He raised both hands before his face. His fingers were trembling.

They were the hands of a mortal man. The skin was pale and clean, yet beneath the translucent surface ran faint, delicate veins—dark and still, carrying no visible light. There were nails, there were knuckles, there was blood circulating through living tissue. This vessel had boundaries. He was bound within a fragile shell of flesh.

He parted his lips to speak, yet all that escaped was a broken, raspy murmur. His tongue felt thick and heavy, like clay unworked by speech.

The timber door creaked softly as it opened inward.

A young girl in a simple grey linen dress entered with cautious, measured steps. In her hands, she carried a round wooden tray bearing a steaming clay bowl and a clean white cloth.

She stopped in her tracks the moment she saw the figure sitting upright upon the cot.

Her bright brown eyes widened, yet no cry of fear escaped her. Her gentle features softened, letting out a long, quiet breath of relief.

"You're... you're awake?" she whispered softly.

Approaching the cot, she set the tray upon a three-legged stool and pulled up a low wooden seat beside him. Her movements were calm, practiced in the quiet art of the sickroom.

"Don't move too fast," she whispered, her warm fingers gently brushing the back of his hand. "Your body was frozen to the bone last night. Grandpa and my father had to light both hearths just to drive the frost from your clothes."

He stared into her face. A gentle, round countenance framed by hazel hair braided over one shoulder, and a pair of limpid, brown eyes.

*Mira.*

The name stirred in the mist of his recovering mind. The name he had whispered before consciousness surrendered upon the stone cliff.

Mira lifted the pottery bowl, stirring its contents with a carved wooden spoon. The fragrant steam of wheat broth mingled with sweet roots and warming spices drifted through the chamber.

"This is Grandpa's healing brew," Mira said, holding a spoonful toward him. "You need your strength back. Come on, drink up."

He stared at the wooden spoon with hesitant wonder. He reached out to take it himself, yet his stiff, unpracticed fingers failed to grip the smooth wood. The spoon tilted, spilling a warm droplet of broth onto the coarse woolen blanket.

He froze. His gaze locked onto the damp stain, his breath hitching as though he had committed an unpardonable disruption of cosmic order.

Mira offered a small, reassuring smile. Without the slightest trace of irritation, she dabbed the spill clean with her cloth.

"It's all right," she murmured gently. "Let me help you."

As the warm broth touched his tongue, rich, savory, and soothing heat trickled down his throat. The primal pang of mortal hunger—a sensation entirely foreign to an eternal concept—bloomed in his chest, sending vitality through his stiff limbs.

"M... Mi... ra..." he rasped.

Tears glistened in Mira's eyes. "Yes, it's me. You're safe here. This is my grandpa's house—he's the Village Elder. No one's going to hurt you."

Just then, hurried footsteps thudded across the timber floor outside. The door banged wide open.

"Mira! Is he—whoa, he's actually awake!"

A boy bounded into the room. His brown hair was a tangled nest of dry pine needles, his hunting leather streaked with mud, and a notched wooden practice sword was thrust through his hemp belt. It was Kael.

"Kael!" Mira scolded sharply, setting the bowl down. "How many times do I have to tell you not to kick the door open? He only just woke up!"

Kael grinned broadly, rushing to the side of the cot with undisguised hero worship.

"Hey! You can hear me, right, brother?" Kael exclaimed, his eyes shining. "You were incredible last night! You took that purple fiend's claws barehanded! But then you dropped like a felled tree. My father had to carry you all the way down the ridge on his shoulders!"

Azariel blinked slowly, overwhelmed by the torrent of boyish vitality.

"Kael, give him some room!" Mira tugged at the hem of Kael’s hunting vest.

"I just wanted to see how he was doing, Mira!" Kael tapped the hilt of his wooden sword with immense pride. "Listen to me, brother! My father's in command of the border watch now. And starting this morning, he's drilling us in the western paddock. I'm learning how to block and strike!"

Kael dropped into an awkward, eager combat stance. His technique was raw, but his eyes burned with resolute fire.

"So you don't have to worry about anything! Grandpa told me my main duty is to guard the two of you here. That violet-flamed monster will have to step over my dead body before it ever touches this room!"

Looking into the fierce devotion burning in the young hunter's eyes, the deep frost within the stranger's chest began to thaw. In his ancient memories, mortals had always regarded his presence with rigid dread or bowed submission. Yet these two children... they saw him merely as a wounded protector in need of shelter and care.

The corners of his lips curved faintly, forming the fragile shadow of a smile.

"Look, Mira! He smiled at me!" Kael cheered gleefully.

"Of course he did, with you acting so silly," Mira retorted, shaking her head, though a fond smile lit her own face.

***

The days that followed slipped by with peaceful rhythm in the secluded valley.

The Village Elder visited the small room at every sunrise and dusk. The silver-bearded patriarch never pressed for his origins or nature. With the serene warmth of a grandfather, he would sit beside the straw cot, rest a hand upon the young man's shoulder, and address him by a new name:

*"Azariel... God’s Aid to a wounded earth."*

The name settled into his consciousness, rooting his newborn existence into the mortal world.

From the rear porch where he was soon permitted to sit and breathe the crisp mountain air, Azariel observed the pulse of the agrarian settlement.

Each dawn, Mira’s father—a quiet, sinewy farmer—set out for the wheat fields with sickle and spade slung over his back. Before leaving, the man would tenderly smooth his daughter's hair and offer a shy, respectful nod toward Azariel. Meanwhile, at the western edge of the village, Bran drilled the local youth in spear and sword formations; Azariel watched Kael tumble repeatedly into the wet grass, scramble up with a grimace, and swing his practice blade until sweat soaked his brow.

And beside Azariel was Mira, patiently guiding him into the world of men.

Upon the low timber table of the porch, Mira placed a basin of spring water and strips of cured birch bark. The rhythmic scrape of stone against stone drew Azariel's attention—Mira grinding dried herbs in a small mortar, the sound steady and oddly absorbing. Then the released scent reached him. Sharp, green, medicinal—almost eye-wateringly pungent. He leaned back slightly without meaning to, blinking.

Mira caught the small recoil and let out a soft laugh. "Too strong? You'll get used to it. These herbs are what kept your fever from getting worse."

While grinding, she spoke aloud the names of the things around them.

Staring at his own reflection in the water basin, Azariel lingered in long silence. His hair was silver-white, brushed with vivid azure at the tips. His complexion was fair and unblemished, his pupils glowing with a subtle, pale-blue ember. He touched his hair with hesitant fingers. It was soft, fragile, and real.

"That's your hair, Azariel," Mira remarked with a soft chuckle. "The color of a falling star."

Picking up a charcoal stick, Mira sketched three ancient script symbols onto the smooth birch bark.

"This is the script of Aethelgard," Mira explained. "This is *Earth*, this is *Water*, and this is *Fire*."

Mira offered the charcoal. Azariel took it between his fingers. Yet when he pressed the tip against the bark, the brittle stick snapped instantly under his unconscious pressure, crumbling into black dust.

Azariel froze, staring down at the crushed charcoal in his palm. Deep guilt shadowed his eyes. Hands once accustomed to anchoring the fabric of creation were now too uncalibrated to govern the fragile things of earth.

Mira merely laughed softly, sweeping away the black dust and pressing a fresh piece into his hand.

"Not so hard, Azariel. Gently, like touching a young leaf," she murmured, resting her hand softly over his wrist to guide him.

Five times he failed, the lines fracturing or running too thick. On the sixth attempt, his hand shifted on the charcoal—too much pressure again. The stick began to give. He felt the micro-fracture forming beneath his grip, the same crumbling that had swallowed the five attempts before. He held his breath. Consciously, deliberately, he loosened his hold—just enough, just barely—and pushed the tip across the bark with measured restraint. The line held. A fourth character formed upon the bark: straight, balanced, and perfectly poised.

Mira stared at the glyph, her eyes wide with wonder. "The lines... they're so neat," she whispered in amazement. "It took me weeks just to draw the curves evenly."

Azariel gazed upon the script in silence. On a shelf along the porch wall leaned an ancestral prayer scroll belonging to the Elder. In the archaic tongue he was learning to read, the text described the Envoy as an unbending authority demanding absolute mortal submission. His fingers tightened around the edge of the bark strip, the memory of the white sky pressing in behind his eyes—cold, vast, pitiless. The blades of cleansing. The screaming below him. The agony of shielding something that was not supposed to be shielded.

A silent, wordless question settled within his heart.

That night, within the small chamber washed in the amber glow of the oil lamp, mountain drafts whispered against the timber walls. Kael lay fast asleep upon the front veranda, exhausted from a long day of blade-work.

Beside Azariel’s cot, Mira had drifted off leaning against her wooden stool, her breathing soft and rhythmic, a damp cloth resting forgotten in her lap. The thin woolen shawl covering her shoulders had slipped to the floorboards.

Azariel rose quietly from the bed. His bare feet made no sound against the wooden planks. Bending down, he lifted the wool blanket from the floor and draped it tenderly over the shoulders of the girl who had nursed him back to life.

He looked at his own hands beneath the dim lamplight. Skin. Blood. A heartbeat. He pressed his palm to his chest and simply stood there, learning the rhythm.

Stepping to the window, he looked out toward the distant silhouette of Pine Cliff against the stars. His brow furrowed faintly. Something lay waiting out there—beyond the trees, beyond the dark. He did not yet have the words for it. He only knew that it was cold, and that it remembered him.
