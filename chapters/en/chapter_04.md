# Chapter 4: Beneath the Shade of the Old Fig Tree

Seven summers had rolled across the golden expanse of the border valley.

Heads of ripe wheat swayed in unison to the gentle mountain drafts, releasing the sweet perfume of fertile loam and sun-cured hay. At the heart of the vast fields stood a colossal fig tree, its massive buttress roots anchoring into the earth like the claws of an ancient dragon. Within the living memory of the village elders, the tree had never borne a single fruit, yet its dense, emerald canopy spread wide into an immense pavilion that defied the blazing sun.

Reclining against the hollow of those buttress roots, Azariel rested in the cool shade.

His fingers, once stiff and alien to physical form, turned the brittle parchment of a weathered leather-bound tome with practiced care. His silver-white hair, still kissed with vivid azure at every tip, remained hidden beneath a simple cowl of brown linen—a disguise urged upon him by the Village Elder so that the itinerant merchants who occasionally passed through would see him only as a quiet, adopted son of the village patriarch.

As Azariel’s bare palm brushed the furrowed bark of the fig tree, a faint, unnatural tremor vibrated against his skin. The ancient tree—whose taproots plunged deep into the bedrock of the valley—seemed to be catching an alien disturbance traveling through the deep strata of the earth. A cold, ruinous pulse crept through the ancient fibers, a silent alarm of nature that resonated instantly with the quiet ember slumbering within Azariel’s chest.

Azariel paused, studying the dark veins of the timber before the sound of pounding boots broke the serenity of the fields.

"Hey! Azariel, my brother!"

From the eastern embankment, a figure came sprinting through the amber stalks. Kael had grown into a broad-shouldered, sinewy young man of nineteen. His skin had bronzed under years of open skies, and at his belt hung a forged iron blade sheathed in brass-fitted black leather—a genuine weapon of war bequeathed from his father’s past.

"I knew I'd find you hiding out here again!" Kael called out, sliding down onto the grass beside Azariel and wiping sweat from his temple. "It was brutal out on the training field today. Father truly meant to crack my ribs with that wooden staff!"

Azariel lowered his book, offering a quiet smile. "Uncle Bran was only working on your stance, Kael. From here, your strike looked steadier than ever."

"Steady? My legs are shaking like a newborn fawn's," Kael grumbled with mock misery, though a flicker of boyish pride shone in his eyes. "You haven't eaten yet, have you?"

"Of course he hasn't, Kael. Who else would bring him lunch if not me?"

A composed, melodic voice drifted from the foot trail. Mira stepped gracefully from the shade of the tall stalks. Azariel's eyes found her the way they always did—drawn first to the steadiness of her gait, then to the quiet efficiency of her hands. In the seven years since she had knelt beside him on a cold crag and pressed her cloak against his chest, she had grown into something that the scroll-words he had learned could barely contain. Not merely older. More settled. The way she moved through the wheat—already scanning his face for signs of strain before she had even said hello—was a habit she had built the way a healer builds all habits: quietly, out of refusal to let someone suffer unnoticed. Slung over her shoulder was the leather satchel of poultices, tinctures, and bone needles she was never without, while in her hands she carried a woven wicker basket covered with a clean linen cloth.

"Sit up properly, Kael," Mira scolded gently, spreading the linen cloth over a flat root.

From the basket, Mira produced three thick rounds of crusty wheat bread still venting fragrant steam, a bowl of goat's milk cheese, and a chilled earthenware flagon of wild blackberry juice.

"Here, El. Father baked them fresh this morning," Mira said with a gentle smile, passing the warmest loaf to Azariel before handing one to Kael.

The three ate together beneath the sanctuary of the giant fig tree. It was their unbroken tradition through the passing years; the place where Kael shared his sparring triumphs, Mira detailed her herbal discoveries, and Azariel listened with quiet, profound gratitude.

"Brother," Kael said around a mouthful of crust, eyeing the heavy volume in Azariel's lap, "will you come check the snares in the woods with me this afternoon?"

"I have to return this geography book to the village archive first, Kael. I just finished reading it."

"Wait, you finished that already?!" Kael stared in disbelief. "You only borrowed that boulder of a book from Mother two days ago!"

Azariel smiled faintly without disputing the fact.

Kael slumped back with a theatrical groan. "Ugh... does it have to be this afternoon?"

Mira shot Kael a wry glance. "What's the matter? The archive is barely a stone's throw from the council hall. Since when is our brave border watchman terrified of setting foot in a library?"

"I'm not terrified!" Kael countered defensively, scratching the back of his neck. "It's just... the terrain in that archive is treacherous whenever Mother is minding the desk!"

Mira burst into laughter. "Aunt Elora is your own mother!"

"That's exactly why!" Kael muttered.

Azariel rose, brushing dry grass from his tunic. "If you'd rather not, you can wait here, Kael. Mira and I can go together."

"Not a chance!" Kael leaped up, resting his hand upon his sword hilt. "A knight never lets his friends travel unescorted. Let's go!"

Mira smiled warmly, packing their basket. Side by side, the three traversed the earthen dikes through the golden wheat, heading toward the heart of the settlement.

---

The village archive and scriptorium stood peacefully beneath a grove of ancient oaks. The stilt structure of fragrant cedarwood radiated cool tranquility. As Azariel pushed the heavy timber door inward, the familiar scent of aged paper, beeswax, and wood varnish welcomed them.

Behind a long oak desk laden with scroll cases and codices, a middle-aged woman was diligently copying village records. She wore a faded blue gown with a fine white shawl draped over her shoulders. Her gentle, refined countenance bore the quiet dignity of an educated woman who had dedicated her life to preserving the written word of her people.

At the creak of the hinges, she lifted her gaze from the parchment.

"Mother..." Kael squeaked, instinctively taking half a step behind Azariel's shoulder.

Yet the eyes of a mother never missed their mark.

Elora’s face lit up in pure delight. "Kael! My sweet boy!"

Rising with graceful haste, she rounded the desk, her slender fingers immediately reaching up to brush the unruly brown hair from Kael's forehead before fussing over his collar.

"Look at you... Bran must have pushed you mercilessly on the western paddock again. Come, I have a slice of sweet pumpkin pie left in the pantry, let Mother feed you—"

"Mother! Please, stop!" Kael backed away, his face turning beet-red as he cast an embarrassed glance toward the window. "I'm nineteen years old! I'm a candidate for the border patrol's second-in-command!"

Elora’s melodious laughter rang like bells through the quiet room. "Whether you become a patrol commander or a high general of the realm, Kael, to my eyes you will always be the little boy who wailed inconsolably when a bee stung your nose in the melon patch."

Mira muffled her giggles behind her hand, while Azariel smiled warmly at the familiar affection of the hunter’s household.

"Mira, thank you for making sure this stubborn boy ate his midday meal," Elora said kindly.

"Always, Aunt Elora," Mira replied with a respectful curtsy.

Elora turned her attention to Azariel, her eyes gleaming with genuine admiration.

"Good day, Aunt Elora," Azariel said, stepping forward to present the book. "I have come to return this geographical treatise. The cartography and notes on the realm were extraordinary."

Elora accepted the heavy tome, shaking her head in astonishment. "Merciful heavens, Azariel... you finished it already? Your loan period had two full weeks remaining, child. A tome of this density would take an imperial clerk months to digest."

She set the volume upon the desk with a pleased smile.

Azariel glanced toward the wooden shelves behind her, noticing the thin layer of dust gathering across rows of aged, yellowed manuscripts.

"Aunt Elora..." Azariel inquired gently, "have any new manuscripts, realm chronicles, or traveler's journals arrived from the border towns this season?"

At his words, the warmth in Elora’s expression slowly waned. A delicate furrow creased her brow, replaced by genuine sorrow and disquiet.

The archivist let out a long sigh, resting her palms against the timber desk.

"I am sorry to say, Azariel... none," Elora replied softly. "In fact, it is not merely books that have failed to arrive. Three days ago, a dispatch horse from the northern frontier arrived at our outer palisade without a rider. Its saddle was caked in frozen ash, and its reins were severed. Since that dawn, not a single salt wagon or cloth merchant has crossed the northern gap."

Kael frowned, stepping forward. "A riderless courier horse? The northern pass is cut off?"

"No one knows for certain what's happening in the mountain gorges," Elora murmured, gazing out the window toward the northern ridges. "The last dispatch whispered that the stone pass has frozen over and grown perilous. The trade caravans refuse to brave the crossing into our valley."

"Could it just be a rockslide from the storms, Aunt Elora?" Mira asked anxiously.

"Perhaps a rockslide, or perhaps military friction between the Duke and foreign realms," Elora sighed with unease. "Your grandfather and Bran have doubled the guard at the outer watchpost since yesterday."

Elora patted Azariel’s hand reassuringly. "Do not be disheartened, Azariel. You are always welcome to reread the historical scrolls of the Remnant on the eastern shelf if you wish."

"Thank you, Aunt Elora," Azariel murmured with a polite nod.

Yet beneath his calm demeanor, a silent, icy jolt traveled down Azariel’s spine.

It was not the chill of mountain weather. The frigid tremor was crawling down from the north, humming in direct resonance with the quiet ember locked within his chest.

It was no rockslide that had barred the road. The panicked courier horse and the vanishing caravans were signs that the dark shadows from Pine Cliff all those years ago were no longer dormant. The hollow shells—*The Husks*—were creeping toward the border, cutting the arteries of this peaceful valley.

Azariel looked through the archive window, past the golden wheat toward the ragged, grey-blue silhouette of the northern peaks looming in the distance.

---

That evening, after taking leave of the library and parting ways with Kael and Mira at the village crossroad, Azariel did not return directly to the Elder’s house.

Alone, the silver-haired youth walked to the northernmost edge of the wheat fields. There, beneath a dusk sky smoldering like tarnished copper, the wind gusted fiercely, carrying an unnatural, freezing vapor.

Azariel knelt upon the stony ground. His bare fingers pressed against the bedrock. Beneath his palm, the earth felt cold as glacial ice, and the faint stench of ozone and spent ash drifted down from the mountain pass.

Before Azariel could rise, another vibration traveled through the stone.

It was no arcane resonance, but the rapid, frantic drumming of iron horseshoes driven without mercy. The thud of galloping mounts shattered the silence from the winding mountain road.

Azariel pulled his cowl low, slipping swiftly into the dense brush along the embankment. His sharp gaze pierced the deepening twilight.

Five warhorses broke through the gorge gap, staggering in exhaustion. The mounts were lathered in white sweat, their mouths frothing, their breath wheezing in hoarse gasps. Astride them sat riders clad in iron armor that was battered, dented, and blackened by soot. Their cloaks hung in tatters, and upon their breastplates was embossed the official seal of the Duchy of Leovard—a winged lion clawed deep by violent talons.

The column did not halt upon the main road, but veered sharply toward the timber palisade of the village's outer watchpost.

There, Bran and two young patrolmen stood braced with spears and unstrung bows. Yet the moment Bran recognized the leading rider, the stern tension in the hunter's jaw collapsed into stark shock.

"Hold your weapons!" Bran bellowed to his men, stepping swiftly to meet the riders. "Garrett?!"

The man astride the foremost mount slumped from the saddle. His neck gorget hung askew, and his grizzled, grey-bearded face was deathly pale beneath a mask of grime and dried blood. Captain Garrett—Bran’s ancient comrade from his service in the Leovard High Guard—nearly hit the dirt had Bran not caught his shoulders.

"By the heavens... Bran..." Garrett gasped, his voice trembling with horror yet unquenched. "Give my men water... and shelter tonight. Our mounts cannot take another stride."

"What happened in the northern pass, Garrett?" Bran asked in a low, steely voice, his eyes darting to the remaining riders who sat motionless in their saddles with hollow, shell-shocked stares. "The courier horse arrived riderless three days ago. Has there been a rebellion of mountain brigands?"

"Not brigands... nor a rockslide," Garrett whispered, clenching Bran’s leather cuirass so fiercely his knuckles turned white. "We were dispatched into the canyon after the salt caravan vanished. What we uncovered there... Bran, that was no slaughter of mortal men."

From the shadows of the brush a dozen paces away, Azariel held his breath, catching every word with piercing clarity.

"The trade wagons were crushed into frozen ash," Garrett continued, his eyes unfocused as he stared back at the mountain ridge. "The corpses of the guards and merchants... they had dried like rotten timber, without a single drop of blood remaining in their veins. Then the purple mist crept down from the crags."

Garrett swallowed with agonizing difficulty, his voice choking. "Those creatures... they walked upright like men, but their chests were hollow cavities and their heads burned with dark purple fire that froze the air! My men’s lances drove clean through them without sound, without pain! We lost four of our finest just breaking free of the gorge."

Bran’s jaw tightened like vice-iron. His gaze locked onto the blackness of the northern pass with a profound dread never before witnessed by anyone in the valley.

"They are creeping south, Bran," Garrett warned with bitter urgency. "The Duchy is in a frenzy. The Duke has ordered full mobilization and will dispatch a massive garrison to sweep and seal this entire frontier within weeks. This peaceful valley lies squarely in their path. Prepare yourselves."

Bran remained silent for several breaths, weighing the catastrophe hovering upon his threshold. Slowly, he offered a grim nod. "Enter the inner quarters, Garrett. Rest your men. We shall bring this before the Village Elder tonight."

The timber gates of the watchpost creaked shut as the battered riders were led inside.

Beyond the palisade, night had fully swallowed the valley. A freezing gale from the north gathered strength, carrying the scent of ozone and dead ash intimately familiar to Azariel’s senses.

Azariel stepped from the shadows. Beneath his linen cowl, his eyes burned with a quiet, azure luminescence.

Garrett’s report confirmed what the ancient fig tree and the frozen soil had told him: *The Husks* were advancing, guided by their ancient hunger for the essence of the Heart of Aethel resting within his chest. And if the Duchy’s legions marched in to purge the monsters, the secret of his existence—and the lives of the innocent villagers—would be extinguished within days.

For a long moment, he did not move.

His gaze drifted back toward the valley—toward the faint, distant warmth of lamplight flickering in the windows of the settlement below. Somewhere in there, a hunter’s son was probably arguing with his mother about whether he was old enough not to be doted on. Somewhere in there, a healer’s satchel was being repacked for the morning. Seven years had taught him what it meant to belong to something mortal and small and entirely worth protecting. He had not known it was possible to carry people inside your chest until they were already there.

He turned, striding swiftly through the dark toward the Elder’s homestead. Tonight, the conversation that would decide the fate of the valley had to begin.
